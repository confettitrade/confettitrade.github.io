Paper, fixed.

What happened to Paper

Paper launched on October 10 with one market, ETH, at 1000x. Every fill came off the Hyperliquid order book. Open interest ran to $3.3 billion. Then one wallet leaned on the ETH book, printed a price the contract believed, and closed a 660x short into it for $1.28 million. It cost about $52,000 in fees. Opens went dark the same day. Open interest fell to $400 million.

The contract was never wrong. It was reading the wrong number.

What Confetti reads instead

Every fill here is the HyperCore oracle price, the validator median of external venues; the book settles nothing. You ask, and a keeper fills you a few seconds later at the print the oracle shows then. If no keeper comes, anyone may fill you at the worse of the two prints until the request expires, so the page asks your wallet to fill it the moment the keeper window closes. Leverage is set per coin: up to 1000x on DOGE, FARTCOIN, VIRTUAL, WIF, MELANIA, FLOKI, PENGU and PEOPLE, up to 250x on TRUMP and PEPE.

A position carries at most $25,000 of notional ($10,000 on the 250x coins), pays out at most 50 times its margin, and anyone can liquidate it past its bust price for 0.5% of the margin. If the house loses a tenth of itself in an hour, opens pause until a human clears it; the contracts cannot be upgraded.

The fill keeper is run by the house. It fills inside its window, 5 to 8 seconds after you ask, at the oracle print it sees then; which print inside those 3 seconds is its call, worth about one oracle step. After the window anyone may fill you at the worse of the two prints until the request expires at 60 seconds, and the page offers your own wallet first. Late fills are logged.

The price path is three hops. The Exchange asks the PriceOracle for the HyperCore oracle price, the PriceOracle asks the PrecompilePriceSource, and that reads the oracle precompile at 0x0807, the validator median of external venues. The source is fixed in the PriceOracle at deploy and has no setter; the precompile addresses are constants. The oracle's owner can set a market's display clamp between 5 and 100 bps, nothing on the settlement path. The Exchange's owner can add markets, set leverage, the fee from 0 to 5%, the fill windows, the caps and the impact curve within bounds, pause and resume opens, name keepers, and retire a market at the live print, or at a stated price once its feed is dead. A position keeps the deadband, cap and fee it was opened with. Nobody can change a live price, touch a balance or upgrade the code.

Up

The house opens at zero and is built from losses. Each dollar lost mints 100 CONFETTI to the wallet that lost it, until the house has taken its first $250,000. After that the rate decays and does not climb back on its own (the owner can retune the curve within bounds). Staked CONFETTI collects every dollar the house holds above $500,000. The fee is 5% of what you win or lose. Refer someone and 15% of their fees are yours. The team mints 10% on top of every mint.

The house pays wins from what it holds. When that is thin, a win is paid as a queued claim, first in first out, as losses refill the house. The Status tab on the trade page shows the balance and the queue.

The house runs market-activity bots of its own, from its own wallets with house money.

They are left out of volume, open interest, 24h stats and the leaderboard.

Paper did $3.3 billion on one coin. There are ten here, and memes move.

Points

Everything you do here earns points: deposits, opens, closes, wins, losses, liquidations, staking CONFETTI, showing up each day, a streak, a first trade in a new market, volume milestones. Referrals pay the most: 40% of everything your referees earn, for good, 5,000 when each one makes a first trade, and a bonus at 1, 5, 20 and 100 referred traders. Your total sits in the header and ticks when something lands; the Points panel on the trade page shows where it comes from, your link and the top 50. The rules are in points-rules.json.
