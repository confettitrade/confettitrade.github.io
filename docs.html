Paper, fixed.

What happened to Paper

Paper launched on October 10 with two markets, BTC and ETH, at 1000x. Every fill came off the Hyperliquid order book. Open interest ran to $3.3 billion. Then one wallet bought about 6,060 ETH on the Hyperliquid book, printed a price the contract believed, and closed its Paper longs into it for $1.28 million. It cost about $52,000 in fees. Opens never went dark; the caps came down the next day. Open interest fell to $400 million.

The contract was never wrong. It was reading the wrong number.

What Confetti reads instead

Every fill here is the HyperCore oracle price, the validator median of external venues; the book settles nothing. You ask, and a keeper fills you a few seconds later at the print the oracle shows then. If no keeper comes, anyone may fill you at the worse of the two prints until the request expires, so the page asks your wallet to fill it the moment the keeper window closes. Leverage is set per coin: up to 1000x on BTC, ETH, DOGE, FARTCOIN, VIRTUAL, WIF, MELANIA, FLOKI, PENGU and PEOPLE, up to 250x on TRUMP and PEPE.

Every limit is sized to the house's free cash and checked in the block you open: one position, one side of a coin, the whole book. The ticket shows the biggest position you can open right now. What a position can win is fixed when it opens: a multiple of its margin, or a share of the house's free cash if that is smaller. Anyone can liquidate it past its bust price for 0.5% of the margin. A breaker can pause new trades for the rest of an hour that drains house cash. The contracts cannot be upgraded.

On a win you pay the impact haircut, then the win fee on what is left. On a loss you pay nothing beyond the loss. An open fills a small spread past the oracle print; the bust price is measured from the print. Held past the free hours, a position pays a borrow fee for each whole hour, taken from its payout at close. The trade page shows every rate.

The fill keeper is run by the house. It fills inside its window, 5 to 8 seconds after you ask, at the oracle print it sees then; which print inside those 3 seconds is its call, worth about one oracle step. After the window anyone may fill you at the worse of the two prints until the request expires at 60 seconds, and the page offers your own wallet first. Late fills are logged.

Trade without popups: one wallet signature gives this browser a key that can open, close and cancel for you up to a cap per trade, for at most 30 days, and never withdraw. Revoke ends it.

USDC on Hyperliquid comes straight in: send it to the address in the Deposit dialog, then Sweep.

The price path is three hops. The Exchange asks the PriceOracle for the HyperCore oracle price, the PriceOracle asks the PrecompilePriceSource, and that reads the oracle precompile at 0x0807, the validator median of external venues. The source is fixed in the PriceOracle at deploy and has no setter; the precompile addresses are constants. A market's display clamp sits between 5 and 100 bps, nothing on the settlement path. Within fixed bounds, markets can be added and leverage, the fees, the spread, the borrow rate, the fill windows, the limits and the impact curve adjusted; opens can be paused and resumed, keepers named, and a market retired at the live print, or at its last settlement price once its feed is dead. A position keeps the terms it was opened with. Nobody can change a live price, touch a balance or upgrade the code.

Up

The house adds and withdraws its money at any time, down to what it owes queued winners and fees already earned. A win the house cash cannot cover waits in the queue.

The queue is paid first in, first out, from losses and house deposits; while it is not empty, every new win joins it. The House page shows free cash, the queue and every house deposit and withdrawal.

FETTI is the same token as before, and losses here mint none. Staked FETTI earns the stakers' share of the fees. A v3 stake moves over with one button on the $FETTI page. Claims land in your wallet. Refer someone and a share of their fees is yours.

The house runs market-activity bots of its own, from its own wallets with house money.

They count in volume, open interest and the leaderboard. Not in points.

Paper did $3.3 billion on two coins. There are twelve here, and memes move.

Trade

Points

Everything you do here earns points: deposits, opens, closes, wins, losses, liquidations, staking FETTI, showing up each day, a streak, a first trade in a new market, volume milestones. Referrals pay the most: 40% of everything your referees earn, for good, 5,000 when each one makes a first trade, and a bonus at 1, 5, 20 and 100 referred traders. Your balance sits in the green bar at the top of every page, with what you earned today, and ticks when something lands; the Points panel on the trade page shows where it comes from, your link and the top 50. The rules are in points-rules.json.

Points move between wallets. Send in the bar takes an address and a whole number of points and calls the PointsRelay on HyperEVM, a contract with no owner and no storage that only emits the transfer as an event. The indexer reads those events in block order. A transfer counts when the sender's balance at that block (earned, minus sent, plus received) covers the amount. House wallets cannot send or receive. Anything else is ignored and listed under rejected in points.json with its transaction hash. Referral shares, daily caps and streaks run on earned points only, so received points pay no one. The leaderboard ranks by balance. Relay: not deployed yet.
