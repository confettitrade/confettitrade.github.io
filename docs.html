How Confetti works

Synthetic perpetuals on meme coins, up to 1000x, settled against a house that is funded only by losses. Everything below is enforced by immutable contracts on HyperEVM.

Numbers with a dotted green underline are read live from the contracts. Everything else is the spec. Fees are hard-coded and have no setter.

1What it is

Confetti is a house, not an exchange. There is no order book and no counterparty on the other side of your trade except the protocol's own liquidity pool, which we call the LP. When you open a position you are betting against the LP at the mid price of Hyperliquid's order book, read on-chain at that moment. When you close, the same kind of price settles you.

The LP starts at zero and nobody can deposit into it. It grows only when traders lose, and it pays winners only out of what earlier losers left behind. The mechanism is the one Papertrade published for BTC and ETH; this is a fresh implementation of it, written for the meme-coin perpetuals Hyperliquid already quotes, with the extra guards those markets need.

Five rules cover most of it:

  1. One price for everyone. Every open, close and liquidation settles at the clamped Hyperliquid mid. No spread, no funding, no fee on notional, no slippage.
  2. Positions are discrete. Margin and leverage are fixed when you open. You close in full or you get liquidated. There are no top-ups, partial closes or edits.
  3. Wins pay a haircut. A winning close is scaled down by an impact curve that bites hardest on small moves, then pays a 2% fee. Losses pay nothing extra: a 2% fee is carved out of what the LP gains.
  4. Winners wait if the LP is dry. Margin always comes back immediately. Profit the LP cannot pay goes into a first-in, first-out queue and is paid as new losses arrive.
  5. Losers get CONFETTI. Every dollar lost mints CONFETTI on a flat-then-decaying curve. Staked CONFETTI collects half of all fees plus every dollar the LP holds above a cap.

2Price feed

Prices come from the HyperCore precompiles: contracts at fixed addresses that let HyperEVM read Hyperliquid's own prices in the same block. The price oracle calls two of them for every execution price and exposes a third for display, each with abi.encode(uint32 assetIndex).

PrecompileAddressReturnsUsed for
BBO0x000000000000000000000000000000000000080e(uint64 bid, uint64 ask)Execution price (the mid)
Oracle0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000807uint64 oracleSafety clamp (validator median of external venues)
Mark0x0000000000000000000000000000000000000806uint64 markDisplay only (markPrice); never used to settle

Raw values are integers with 6 − szDecimals implied decimals, where szDecimals is the asset's size precision on Hyperliquid. The oracle brings them to 1e18 fixed point and takes the mid:

Execution price
price$ = raw / 10(6 − szDecimals)
mid = bid + ask2
lo = oracle × (1 − D) , hi = oracle × (1 + D)
execution = min( hi, max( lo, mid ) ) D is the market's clamp half-width in bps, set by tier

The clamp is the one safety feature a meme-coin house cannot skip, and what it is anchored to matters. Hyperliquid's mark price is partly derived from Hyperliquid's own book (its best bid and ask, last trade and a short average of the mid), so a sustained push on a thin book drags the mark along, and a band around the mark would drift with the push. The oracle price is a validator median of external exchanges; a push on one venue does not move it. Confetti clamps to the oracle price, so however far the book is pushed, the execution price moves at most D from what the rest of the market says the coin is worth, and a manufactured win is worth at most about D in price. The band bounds the price, not liquidations: it keeps a push from reaching the bust move only when D is narrower than that move, which is why tier A uses 5 bps against the 9.5 bps bust of a 1000x position (tier B: 25 vs 38 bps, tier C: 50 vs 190). It reduces the attack, it does not remove it (see Risks).

If the BBO or the oracle call fails or returns zero, the price oracle reverts (a dead mark feed changes nothing). There is no stale-price fallback and no last-known value: the market is simply untradeable until the feed is back, and that includes closes, liquidations and the guardian's retire. If a feed is dead for good, because Hyperliquid delisted the perp, the owner can retire the market at a stated price so that open margins are not locked forever; the contract accepts that call only while the feed is dead, so it can never override a live price (section 7). Opens, closes and liquidations all use the same clamped price, so there is no second price to argue about.

3Markets and tiers

A 1000x position is liquidated by a 9.5 bps adverse move, so a market only qualifies for 1000x if its spread is far below that. Every market has separate long and short open-interest caps in USD notional; the market keeper moves them within a ceiling per tier ($2M / $500K / $100K), and an open that would push a side past its cap reverts. On top of that, no single position may exceed the tier's notional cap (owner-set, at most $100,000), and the profit paid on one position is capped at payoutCapX times its margin (50x at launch, owner-set between 10x and 200x); anything above the cap stays with the LP. Each market also carries the parameters of its impact curve (section 5): base rate 10%, rate multiplier 15,000, reference notional $20,000, and a position multiplier fixed per market at deploy so that a 1% move keeps about 88% of its raw gain and a 0.1% move about 74% (130,000,000 for the reference curve).

4Positions

Collateral is plain USDC. deposit() moves it into your available balance on the Exchange; withdraw() sends available balance back to your wallet. Opening a position moves margin from available into the position; closing moves it back, plus or minus the result.

Open

open(instrumentId, isLong, marginUsdc, leverage) succeeds when all of these hold:

  • margin is at least 10 USDC and you have it available;
  • leverage is a whole number from 1 to the market's tier maximum;
  • notional = margin × leverage is at most the market's per-position cap and fits under that side's open-interest headroom;
  • the market exists, is not retired, opens are not paused for it or globally, and the LP breaker is not tripped.

Entry is the clamped mid at that block. Nothing is charged to open.

Bust price

A position busts when the adverse move reaches 95% of what would wipe its margin. The 5% buffer means a liquidation always lands before the voluntary close path would have returned zero, so the LP never has to chase a trader for more than their margin.

Bust price
long: entry × ( 1 − 0.95leverage ) · short: entry × ( 1 + 0.95leverage )

Close

close(positionId) can only be called by the owner, only for the whole position, and settles at the current clamped mid using the rules in section 5.

Liquidate

liquidate(positionId) can be called by anyone once the clamped mid is at or beyond the bust price. The whole margin is forfeited: 0.5% goes to the caller as a bounty, a 2% loss fee goes to the fee pool (while the queue is empty), and the rest goes to the LP. CONFETTI is minted to the position's owner on the full margin.

There are no margin top-ups, no partial closes, no cross-margin and no edits. A retired market has its price frozen; positions in it can only be closed or liquidated at that frozen price.

5Settlement

Every close is computed from four numbers: the entry price, the exit price, the notional and the direction.

Definitions
notional = margin × leverage
move = | exit − entry |entry
rawPnl = notional × ( exitentry − 1 ) × dir dir = +1 for a long, −1 for a short

Losing close

When rawPnl is negative the loss is capped at the margin. You get the rest of your margin back at once; the LP gains the loss.

Losing close (rawPnl < 0)
loss = min( margin, −rawPnl )
returned now = margin − loss
queue empty: fee = 2% × loss → fee pool , LP gains loss − fee , CONFETTI mints on loss − fee
queue active: no fee , LP gains loss , CONFETTI mints on loss

No deadband and no impact apply to a loss. The only thing taken from a loss is the 2% fee, and it is carved out of the LP's gain, not added to what you pay.

Winning close

A win goes through five steps, in this order.

  1. Deadband. The exit price is moved deadbandBps against you before the gain is computed. A gain that does not survive the deadband is zero and you simply get your margin back.
  2. Impact. The raw gain is multiplied by a scale that depends only on the size of the move. Small moves keep little; the scale climbs toward 1 − baseRate as the move grows.
  3. Payout cap. The adjusted gain is capped at payoutCapX × margin (50x at launch, the gain of a 5% move at 1000x). Anything above it stays in the LP.
  4. Win fee. 2% of the capped, impact-adjusted gain goes to the fee pool.
  5. Payout. Margin comes back immediately. The profit is paid from LP cash if the LP has it; otherwise it is appended to the queue.
Winning close (rawPnl > 0)
exit′ = exit × (1 − deadband) for a long , exit × (1 + deadband) for a short
scale = 1 − baseRate 1 + 1move × rateMultiplier + referenceNotionalmove × positionMultiplier
adjusted = min( rawPnl × scale , payoutCapX × margin )
fee = 2% × adjusted → fee pool
payout = margin + adjusted − fee move and rawPnl use exit′; referenceNotional in dollars; positionMultiplier is the market's deploy-time constant

The impact curve is what lets the house quote a single mid with no spread: it takes back most of the gain on moves so small that they could be noise or a pushed book, and almost nothing on moves large enough to be real. With the reference parameters the scale works out like this.

Queue and fee pool

All USDC sits in the Exchange. LP cash is what is left after every available balance and every open margin. Four claims compete for it, and they are paid strictly in this order:

  1. The queue. Profit the LP could not pay at close time, oldest first. harvest() is permissionless: anyone can call it to pay the head of the queue as far as LP cash reaches, partial payments included. While the queue is not empty, every new win is queued in full and no loss fee is carved, so losses refill the queue as fast as possible.
  2. Wins at close time. With an empty queue, a win is paid from LP cash on the spot; any shortfall starts a new queue.
  3. The fee pool. pushFees() is permissionless and only works while the queue is empty and LP cash covers the whole pool. It sends 50% to CONFETTI stakers and 50% to the dev fee recipient.
  4. Staker overflow. LP cash above the staker cap, swept by pushStakerFees() (section 6).

Queued profit earns nothing while it waits and has no deadline. It is a claim on future losses, nothing more.

6CONFETTI

CONFETTI is what losers get. Every losing close and every liquidation mints it to the trader who lost, in proportion to what the LP gained from them. It cannot be bought, it cannot be transferred at launch, and the only thing it does is collect fees when staked.

Mint basis and rate

Mint
basis = LP's net gain from the loss (loss − fee while the queue is empty; the whole loss while it is active; the whole margin on a liquidation)
minted = basis × rate

The rate depends on two running numbers in the Tokenomics contract:

  • trackedLpUsd, the LP's cumulative profit and loss: losses add to it, wins and fee payouts subtract from it, and it can be negative while the queue is owed.
  • tailProgressUsd, a one-way high-water mark of cumulative LP gain past the flat threshold. Each loss that lifts trackedLpUsd above both the threshold and its previous level adds that slice. Nothing ever lowers it.
Rate (CONFETTI per $1 of basis)
flat region, trackedLpUsd < $250,000 : rate = flatRate = 100
tail region, otherwise : rate = flatRate × ( SS + tailProgress )2 S = $15,000,000

Because tailProgress never falls, the rate never goes back up once it has decayed, even if the LP later pays out everything it made. A win that pulls trackedLpUsd back under the threshold does switch the next mint back to the flat rate, though, so the curve is flat-then-decaying rather than strictly monotone in time.

Staker cap

Once trackedLpUsd is above the staker cap, everything above it belongs to stakers. pushStakerFees() is permissionless: it sweeps min(trackedLpUsd − cap, LP cash) to the Staking contract, but only while the queue is empty. The sweep debits trackedLpUsd and leaves tailProgress alone, so it does not change the mint rate. The LP therefore never holds more than the cap for long; the cap is the house's working float.

The token

  • ERC-20, 18 decimals, symbol CONFETTI. Minted only by the Exchange, never pre-minted. Total minted so far: —.
  • Transfers are locked. Until the owner calls enableTransfers(), the only moves allowed are mints and transfers to or from the Staking contract. The switch is one-way.

Staking

  • Stake and unstake are instant, with no lockup and no cooldown. Currently staked: —.
  • Dividends are USDC, split pro rata by a per-share accumulator. The accumulator is scaled by 1e30, which gives twelve digits of headroom below one micro-USDC; whole units the split cannot absorb wait in a carry and ride along with the next payment. Total paid to stakers so far: —.
  • claim() does not send USDC to your wallet. It credits your Exchange balance, where you can trade it or withdraw it.
  • Income comes from two places: half of every pushFees(), and all of every staker-cap sweep.

Bounds and constants

  • Max leverage per tier is a constant: 1000x / 250x / 50x.
  • Deadband 1 to 50 bps in whole bps (the spec's 0.5 bps floor rounds up to 1). Clamp 5 to 100 bps. Base rate 5% to 20%. The impact curve as a whole must keep between 50% and 95% of a raw gain at a 1% move, so no setting can quietly zero every win.
  • Notional per position at most $100,000. Payout cap 10x to 200x margin. LP breaker 2% to 50% of the LP per hour; only the guardian or owner clears it. OI caps per side at most $2M / $500K / $100K by tier.
  • Fixed, with no setter: win fee 2%, loss fee 2%, liquidation bounty 0.5%, bust buffer 95%, minimum margin 10 USDC.
  • Tokenomics: the flat rate is fixed for life; the threshold must not exceed the staker cap; S must be positive.

8Risks

Read this part twice.

  • This is a house. Every trade is against the LP, and the LP is funded only by other traders' losses. If traders win net, winners wait in the queue, with no interest and no deadline. A queued profit is a claim on future losses that may never arrive.
  • Thin books can be pushed. The main attack is moving the top of a meme-coin order book to print a price that is not real. The oracle clamp, the per-position caps, the deadband, the impact curve and the tiered leverage all shrink the payoff of doing that, and the LP breaker pauses opens once an hour's net outflow passes its share; none of them makes it impossible.
  • A dead feed freezes everything. If the BBO or oracle precompile stops answering for a market, that market cannot be opened, closed, liquidated or retired by the guardian until it recovers; no last good price is kept. The one way out is the owner, behind its 48-hour timelock, retiring the market at a stated price, which the contract accepts only while the feed is still dead. Until then, margin in that market is locked.
  • Liquidations are permissionless, not guaranteed. A position past its bust price stays open until somebody calls liquidate(), and until then it can come back. The 0.5% bounty exists so that somebody does, but nobody is obliged to.
  • Immutable means unfixable. There is no upgrade path. If a bug is found, the tools are pause, retire and withdraw; there is no patch.
  • CONFETTI is not money. It is non-transferable at launch and is worth exactly what the fees it collects are worth. The mint rate decays and never recovers.
  • Regulation. Synthetic leveraged products are regulated derivatives in most jurisdictions. The operator must take legal advice before launch and geo-fence the frontend; the contracts themselves cannot.
